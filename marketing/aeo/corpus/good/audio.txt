Browsers block audio playback until a page has received a user gesture. An
audio element that calls play on load will have its promise rejected, and the
rejection is silent unless it is explicitly caught. The reliable pattern is to
start the element muted, which is permitted without a gesture, and to unmute on
the first click or keypress.

A listener that removes itself on the first gesture will fire before an
asynchronous fetch completes if the track is loaded as a blob, leaving the
element muted permanently. Guarding the removal behind a flag that is only set
once playback has actually begun avoids that race.

Looping is set on the element rather than by rescheduling playback on the ended
event, because the ended handler introduces an audible gap on every repetition.
