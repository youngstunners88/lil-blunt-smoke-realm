# Three SEO reels — transcripts, and what actually applies here

Source: three Instagram reels supplied by the founder, 2026-09-02.
Full transcripts sit alongside this file (`Dax-sgbD8hs.txt`, `DcuIrN2oJAA.txt`,
`Dak0tDXOkMo.txt`).

## How they were captured (no paid service needed)

`transcriptx.ai` and a custom MCP both turned out to be unnecessary. Public IG
reels download without a login:

```sh
pip3 install yt-dlp
python3 -m yt_dlp -f bestaudio/best -o "%(id)s.%(ext)s" --write-info-json <reel-url>
```

The `.info.json` carries the **full caption**, which for all three contained the
entire argument — often more explicit than the voiceover. Audio was transcribed
with ElevenLabs Scribe:

```sh
curl -X POST https://api.elevenlabs.io/v1/speech-to-text \
  -H "xi-api-key: $ELEVENLABS_API" \
  -F file=@reel.m4a -F model_id=scribe_v1 -F language_code=eng
```

**Key detail:** use `$ELEVENLABS_API`, **not** `$ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` — the latter
lacks the `speech_to_text` permission and 401s. Cost: pennies.

## What each reel actually says

### 1. Colin S (@colinstuckert) — "75 places to get backlinks"

A reaction video scrolling a list: Chrome Web Store, Forbes, GitHub, TrustRadius,
SourceForge, Gumroad, Substack, Yelp, Wikipedia. The suggested tactics include
creating "a little cheap bookmark app that nobody's gonna use" and "a free fake
newsletter" purely to place links.

**Verdict for this project: mostly skip.** The presenter openly does not know if
it works ("I don't actually know how that works… Let me know if it works") — it
is speculation, not a validated playbook. Most of those links are nofollow
profile links, and the fake-newsletter / throwaway-extension tactics are the
kind of manufactured linking Google discounts and that can damage a brand trying
to look legitimate. This also runs straight into `backlink-building`, which
already concluded that authority-chasing is the wrong scoring function here.
The two genuinely useful entries (itch.io, GitHub) we already have.

### 2. Edward Sturm (@edward.builds) — press releases to influence AI answers

The strongest of the three. Claim: a weekly programme of **neutral, factual**
press releases, each written around an **unbranded question buyers ask AI
assistants**, distributed over a newswire, produced 108 repeating AI citations —
78 from a single release. Mechanic: put the target term in the **title, the
subheading, and the opening sentence**; the release ranks and the AI Overview
cites it. Platforms named: AB Newswire (~$500/yr, 83 releases, ≈$6 each, but
they decay in days-to-weeks) and EIN Presswire (~$1,000 for 15, ≈$66 each,
lasting months).

**Verdict: the mechanic is the valuable part, and it is free. The newswire is
paid amplification, not the idea.** Writing neutral, factual, question-shaped
content with the term in title/subhead/first line is something we can do on our
own pages today at zero cost. Note the caveat: those citation numbers are one
vendor's self-reported case, unaudited.

### 3. Thomas Guardado (@coachtommy.g) — three local-SEO moves

Google Business Profile service descriptions, a BrightLocal citation campaign
for consistent name/address/phone, and — the one he says "did the most damage" —
pulling SEMrush Keyword Magic Tool, **filtering by questions, and writing one
page per question**: "Not blog fluff. Real answers."

**Verdict: two-thirds inapplicable, one-third excellent.** Lil Blunt is a browser
game with no premises, so Google Business Profile, the Map Pack and local
citations do not exist for us. But the third move transfers exactly.

## The convergence — and the actual recommendation

Two of the three reels independently land on the **same free play**: find the
real questions people ask, and answer each one on its own page, plainly, with
the question wording in the title and opening line.

That is precisely the AEO work this repo already built — `/about/`,
`/how-to-play/`, `/troubleshooting/`, the FAQ structured data, `llms.txt`, and
`crawl_gate.py` to police it. **So the accelerant is not a new tactic. It is
shipping what already exists**, because a crawl of the live site on 2026-09-02
shows it is not live:

- The homepage is **byte-identical (128,857 bytes) to before this session's
  fixes**. None of the five load-bearing claims are readable without JS.
- `/troubleshooting/` still returns the SPA shell, not a page.
- **`llms.txt` has regressed.** The live file is now 637 bytes of Caffeine's
  generic boilerplate ("a web application built with Caffeine…") — the real
  4,808-byte version describing the game, its controls and its play link was
  replaced by the platform's default. An answer engine reading it today learns
  nothing about the game.

Ranked next actions, all free:

1. **Restore `llms.txt`** — an active regression, and the single cheapest AEO
   asset we own. It is correct in the repo; the deployed copy is not.
2. **Land the pending Caffeine draft** so the five claims and the real
   `/troubleshooting/` page are crawlable. Re-run `crawl_gate.py` to confirm —
   it currently FAILS.
3. **Then** add question-shaped pages (reels 2 and 3's shared play), one real
   question per page, term in title/subhead/first sentence.
4. Revisit paid newswire (reel 2) only if steps 1–3 move a measured number.
   Per `revenue-paths`, traffic against a free game still multiplies to $0, so
   spend buys learning, not return.

## Standing caveat

`blind-spots` check #1 applies: search volume for this category was measured at
roughly zero-to-70/month. SEO effort here has a low measured ceiling regardless
of tactic. The answer-engine angle (reel 2's thesis) is more promising than
classic ranking precisely because it does not depend on that volume — but it
still needs the pages to be readable, which today they are not.
